# Butter-Poached Lobster Tail

## Overview
Restaurant-quality lobster at home. Butter poaching is the most foolproof method - nearly impossible to overcook, incredibly tender and rich. Much easier than boiling or grilling.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **DONE AT 140°F**: 145°F = rubbery, 140°F = perfect tender
- **BUTTER TOO HOT = FRY**: Keep butter 140-180°F, not boiling
- **TIMING TIGHT**: 6-10 minutes total - check at 6 minutes
- **SIZE MATTERS**: 4-6 oz tails ideal (larger = harder to cook evenly)
- **THAW PROPERLY**: Frozen tail needs 24 hours in fridge
- **BUTTERFLY FOR EVEN COOKING**: Helps cook evenly, looks impressive

## Target Temperature
- **Perfect**: 140°F (60°C) - tender, translucent in center
- **Max**: 145°F (63°C) - still good but firmer
- **Overcooked**: 150°F+ - rubbery

## Equipment
- Large skillet or sauté pan
- Instant-read thermometer
- Kitchen shears
- Small saucepan (for clarifying butter, optional)
- Timer

## Ingredients (serves 2)
- 2 lobster tails (4-6 oz each)
- 1 cup (2 sticks / 226g) unsalted butter
- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 lemon, halved
- 1/2 tsp (3g) kosher salt
- Optional: Pinch cayenne or Old Bay seasoning

## Lobster Tail Selection

**Fresh vs Frozen:**
- **Frozen** is often better quality (flash-frozen on boat)
- **Fresh** only if from trusted source, used same day

**Size:**
- **4-6 oz**: Easiest to cook, most forgiving ⭐ RECOMMENDED
- **6-8 oz**: Good, slightly longer cook time
- **8+ oz**: Harder to cook evenly, expensive

**Type:**
- **Cold water** (Maine, Canadian): Sweeter, more tender, expensive
- **Warm water** (Caribbean, Florida): Larger, less expensive, slightly tougher

**What to Look For:**
- Bright, translucent flesh (not opaque white)
- No black spots or discoloration
- Shells intact, not cracked
- Fresh ocean smell (not fishy/ammonia)

## Method

### Step 1: Thaw if Frozen (24 hours ahead)
1. Place frozen tails in refrigerator
2. Thaw 24 hours in fridge
3. 🚨 **DON'T thaw on counter** (food safety risk)
4. Quick thaw: Sealed in plastic, submerge in cold water 30-60 min

### Step 2: Butterfly the Tails (makes cooking even)
1. Using kitchen shears:
   - Cut through top shell lengthwise (center line)
   - Cut through meat but not bottom shell
   - Stop at tail fin
2. Gently pry shell open
3. Loosen meat from bottom shell (slide finger under)
4. Pull meat up and rest on top of shell
5. Press shell halves together underneath
6. Meat sits on top of shell (beautiful presentation)
7. 🚨 **Keep meat attached** at tail end

### Step 3: Season
1. Pat lobster meat DRY with paper towels
2. Sprinkle with 1/4 tsp (1.5g) salt each
3. Optional: Pinch cayenne or Old Bay

### Step 4: Melt Butter (Low and Slow)
1. Place 1 cup (226g) butter in large skillet
2. Add garlic cloves and thyme sprigs
3. Heat over LOW heat
4. Butter should melt slowly (5-7 minutes)
5. Once melted, maintain at 160-180°F (71-82°C)
6. Should have gentle bubbles, not boiling
7. 🚨 **TOO HOT = FRIES** - keep gentle, not boiling
8. Use thermometer to check butter temp

**What Temperature Looks Like:**
- **140-160°F**: Barely moving, small bubbles
- **160-180°F**: Gentle bubbles, simmering ⭐ IDEAL
- **200°F+**: Rapid bubbles, boiling - TOO HOT

### Step 5: Poach Lobster (6-10 minutes)
1. Gently place butterflied tails in warm butter
2. Meat should be submerged (baste if not fully covered)
3. Maintain gentle simmer (160-180°F)
4. **SET TIMER FOR 6 MINUTES**
5. Baste every 2 minutes with butter
6. 🚨 **WATCH CLOSELY**:
   - Meat goes from translucent (raw) to opaque (cooked)
   - Cook until just opaque with slight translucence in center
7. At 6 minutes, check temp:
   - Insert thermometer into thickest part
   - Looking for 135-140°F (57-60°C)
8. If not there, continue 1-2 more minutes, check again

**Timing by Size:**
- **4 oz tail**: 6-7 minutes
- **5-6 oz tail**: 7-9 minutes
- **7-8 oz tail**: 9-11 minutes

### Step 6: Rest Briefly (2 minutes)
1. Remove from butter at 140°F (60°C)
2. Let rest on plate 2 minutes
3. Temp may rise slightly to 142-145°F

### Step 7: Serve with Butter
1. Plate lobster tails
2. Strain warm butter (remove garlic/herbs)
3. Serve butter in small dishes for dipping
4. Add lemon wedges for squeezing
5. Serve immediately

## Perfect Butter-Poached Lobster Indicators
- Internal temp: 140-145°F (60-63°C)
- Meat opaque white with slight translucence
- Tender, not rubbery
- Sweet, buttery flavor
- Meat easily pulls from shell

## Visual Doneness (if no thermometer)
**Raw:**
- Translucent, glassy appearance
- Grayish color

**Perfect (140°F):**
- Mostly opaque white
- Slight translucence in very center
- Firm but tender when pressed

**Overcooked (150°F+):**
- Completely opaque white
- Very firm/rubbery when pressed
- Shrunken in size

## Troubleshooting

**Rubbery lobster:**
- Overcooked (past 145°F)
- Butter too hot (boiled instead of poached)
- Cooked too long
- **Prevention**: Pull at 140°F, monitor butter temp

**Undercooked:**
- Pulled too early (under 135°F)
- Butter not hot enough
- **Fix**: Return to butter for 1-2 more minutes

**Butter separated/broke:**
- Too hot (butter separated)
- Still safe to use, just not emulsified
- **Prevention**: Keep butter 160-180°F

**Tough/Chewy:**
- Way overcooked (160°F+)
- Old lobster
- **Can't fix** - order pizza

## Alternative Cooking Methods

### Broiled (Quick, easy)
1. Butterfly as above
2. Brush with melted butter
3. Broil 4" from heat, 8-10 minutes
4. Pull at 140°F

### Grilled
1. Butterfly, brush with oil
2. Grill flesh-side down 5-6 minutes
3. Flip, grill 2-3 more minutes
4. Pull at 140°F

### Steamed
1. Steam over boiling water
2. 8-10 minutes for 4-6 oz tails
3. Pull when opaque

## Clarified Butter (Optional - Cleaner Presentation)

**Why Clarify:**
- Removes milk solids (no foam)
- Higher smoke point
- Clearer, prettier
- Professional technique

**How to Clarify:**
1. Melt 1.5 cups (3 sticks) butter slowly
2. Let sit 5 minutes (milk solids sink)
3. Skim foam from top
4. Carefully pour clear butter into pan
5. Leave white milk solids at bottom
6. Use clarified butter for poaching

## Pro Tips

**Buying:**
- Buy frozen from reputable source (Costco, quality fishmonger)
- Look for "cold water" label (better quality)
- Calculate 1-2 tails per person (4-6 oz each)

**Prep:**
- Can butterfly day ahead, refrigerate
- Bring to room temp 15 minutes before cooking
- Pat very dry (moisture = poaching instead of gentle cook)

**Cooking:**
- Thermometer is your friend (no guessing)
- Low and slow butter (not boiling)
- Baste frequently (ensures even cooking)

**Serving:**
- Provide lemon wedges
- Small forks for eating
- Extra melted butter for dipping
- Wet towels (lobster is messy!)

**Waste Not:**
- Save shells for shellfish stock
- Freeze shells in bag until ready

## Whole Lobster vs Tails

**Tails:**
- ✅ No waste (all edible meat)
- ✅ Easier to cook
- ✅ No mess
- ✅ Consistent quality frozen
- ❌ More expensive per pound
- ❌ No claw/knuckle meat

**Whole Lobster:**
- ✅ More meat (claws, knuckles)
- ✅ Dramatic presentation
- ✅ Can make stock from body
- ❌ Messy to eat
- ❌ Harder to cook evenly
- ❌ Must buy live or risk quality

**Verdict for Home:** Tails are easier, less messy, more foolproof
