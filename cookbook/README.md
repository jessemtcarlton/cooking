## Fundamental Techniques

### Temperature Control
- **Carryover Cooking**: Meat continues cooking after removal (5-10°F rise)
- **Resting**: Always rest proteins (8-10 min for steaks)
- **Thermometer Use**: Insert through SIDE into center for accurate reading

### Searing
- **Hot Pan**: Must be screaming hot (wait 5 full minutes)
- **Dry Surface**: Pat proteins dry 3x before searing
- **Don't Move**: Let crust form before flipping
- **Oil Choice**: Use high smoke-point oils (avocado, grapeseed)

### Sauce Making
- **Deglazing**: Add liquid to hot pan, scrape fond (brown bits)
- **Reduction**: Simmer to concentrate flavors, thicken
- **Butter Mounting**: Add cold butter off heat, whisk constantly
- **Emulsion**: Keep warm (not hot) or sauce breaks

### Baking
- **Measure Accurately**: Weight is best, especially for flour
- **Room Temperature**: Eggs, butter mix better at room temp
- **Don't Overmix**: Develops gluten, makes tough baked goods
- **Oven Thermometer**: Most ovens are off by 25°F

## Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

### Proteins
❌ **Not using thermometer** → Use one every time, no exceptions
❌ **Cooking from cold** → Bring to room temp 40-60 min before
❌ **Not resting** → Always rest, temp rises and juices redistribute
❌ **Pressing down on meat** → Squeezes out juices, makes dry
❌ **Flipping too often** → Flip once only (unless controlling flare-ups)

### Sides
❌ **Overcrowding pan** → Work in batches for proper browning
❌ **Not preheating oven** → Always preheat fully (15 min)
❌ **Opening oven constantly** → Every time = temp drop, longer cooking
❌ **Using wrong potato type** → Russet or Yukon Gold for mashing, NOT red

### Sauces
❌ **Adding cold butter to hot pan** → Sauce breaks, becomes greasy
❌ **Over-reducing** → Can't add liquid back, tastes burnt
❌ **Leaving garlic in pan too long** → Burns, tastes bitter
❌ **Not seasoning enough** → Taste and adjust salt at end

## Meal Planning Strategies

### Weeknight Steak Dinner (1 hour total)
1. Remove steaks from fridge (40 min before)
2. Start Hasselback potatoes in oven (60 min)
3. Make compound butter (can do days ahead)
4. Start asparagus when steaks go in (12 min)
5. Cook steaks (method of choice)
6. Everything finishes together

### Date Night Impressive Menu (Plan ahead)
- **Day before**: Make crème brûlée (chill overnight)
- **2 hours before**: Prep soufflé bases
- **1 hour before**: Start reverse-sear steak
- **30 minutes before**: Start sides
- **20 minutes before**: Finish steaks, make pan sauce
- **Right before serving**: Brûlée dessert, put soufflés in

### Sunday Meal Prep
- Make compound butters (freeze in logs)
- Make chimichurri (lasts 2 weeks)
- Prep/portion proteins (freeze individually)
- Par-cook potatoes (finish day-of)

## Temperature Reference Guide

### Beef (Pull Temperature / Final After Rest)
- **Rare**: 120°F / 125°F
- **Medium-Rare**: 130°F / 135°F ⭐ RECOMMENDED
- **Medium**: 140°F / 145°F
- **Medium-Well**: 150°F / 155°F
- **Well-Done**: 160°F+ (don't do this to good steak)

### Seafood
- **Salmon**: 120°F / 125-130°F (medium-rare, slightly translucent)
- **Scallops**: 115-120°F (opaque with translucent center)
- **Shrimp**: 120°F (pink and opaque)
- **Lobster**: 135-140°F (opaque throughout)

### Poultry
- **Chicken Breast**: 160°F / 165°F
- **Chicken Thigh**: 170-175°F (higher temp breaks down connective tissue)
- **Duck Breast**: 130-135°F (medium-rare, like steak)

### Pork
- **Pork Chops**: 140°F / 145°F (slightly pink is safe and juicy)
- **Pork Tenderloin**: 140°F / 145°F
- **Pork Shoulder**: 195-205°F (for pulling)

## Troubleshooting Index

### "My steak is overcooked"
- Use thermometer (no guessing)
- Pull 5-10°F below target
- Account for carryover cooking
- Try reverse-sear method (more forgiving)

### "My sauce broke/separated"
- Butter added when too hot
- Whisk in ice cube to cool down
- Or start emulsion over with new egg yolk

### "My scallops are rubbery"
- Cooked too long (90 seconds per side max)
- Weren't dry scallops (buy "dry" only)
- Pan not hot enough

### "My mashed potatoes are gluey"
- Used wrong potatoes (must use russet or Yukon Gold)
- Overmixed (fold gently, don't beat)
- Added cold dairy (must be hot)

### "My soufflé didn't rise"
- Egg whites not stiff enough
- Overmixed when folding
- Opened oven door during baking
