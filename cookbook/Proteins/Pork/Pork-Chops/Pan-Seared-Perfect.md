# Perfect Pan-Seared Pork Chops

## Overview
The most commonly overcooked pork dish. Modern pork is safe at 145°F (with slight pink!), not 160°F like old guidelines. This method gives juicy chops with golden crust.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **SAFE AT 145°F**: USDA updated guidelines in 2011 - 145°F is safe with 3-min rest
- **PULL AT 140°F**: Carryover brings to 145°F perfect
- **COOKING TO 160°F = HOCKEY PUCKS**: Old info, ignore it
- **BRINING MANDATORY**: Pork is lean, dries out easily
- **THICKNESS MATTERS**: Use 1-1.5" thick chops (thinner = instant overcook)

## Target Temperature
- **Pull at**: 140°F (60°C)
- **After resting**: 145°F (63°C) - USDA safe, slightly pink, juicy
- **160°F** = old guidelines, DRY

## Equipment
- Large heavy skillet (cast iron or stainless)
- Instant-read thermometer (MANDATORY)
- Tongs
- Timer

## Ingredients (serves 4)
**For Brine:**
- 4 cups (960ml) water
- 1/4 cup (72g) kosher salt
- 1/4 cup (50g) brown sugar
- 2 cups (480ml) ice

**For Chops:**
- 4 bone-in pork chops, 1-1.5" thick (8-10 oz each)
- 2 tbsp (30ml) neutral oil
- 2 tbsp (28g) butter
- 1 tsp (5g) kosher salt (for seasoning)
- 1/2 tsp (2g) black pepper
- 1 tsp (2g) garlic powder
- 1 tsp (1g) dried thyme or sage
- 3 garlic cloves, smashed
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme

## Method

### Step 1: Brine the Pork (30 minutes to 4 hours)
1. Heat 4 cups (960ml) water in saucepan
2. Dissolve 1/4 cup (72g) salt + 1/4 cup (50g) brown sugar
3. Remove from heat
4. Add 2 cups (480ml) ice to cool rapidly
5. Submerge pork chops in brine
6. Refrigerate 30 minutes minimum (1-4 hours ideal)
7. 🚨 **WHY BRINE:**
   - Lean pork dries out easily
   - Brine adds moisture, seasons throughout
   - Insurance against overcooking
   - Makes almost foolproof

**Timing:**
- **Minimum**: 30 minutes
- **Optimal**: 1-2 hours
- **Maximum**: 4 hours (longer gets too salty)

### Step 2: Dry and Season (10 minutes before cooking)
1. Remove chops from brine, rinse briefly
2. Pat VERY DRY with paper towels
3. Let sit 10 minutes (come to room temp)
4. Pat dry again
5. Season both sides:
   - 1/4 tsp (1.5g) salt per chop (light - brined already)
   - Black pepper
   - Garlic powder
   - Thyme or sage

### Step 3: Preheat Pan (5 minutes)
1. Heat skillet over MEDIUM-HIGH heat
2. Let heat 5 full minutes
3. Add 2 tbsp (30ml) oil
4. Oil should shimmer
5. Tilt to coat pan

### Step 4: Sear First Side (4-5 minutes)
1. Pat chops dry ONE FINAL TIME
2. Place chops in pan (don't crowd - work in batches if needed)
3. Should sizzle aggressively
4. 🚨 **DON'T MOVE** - crust won't form
5. **SET TIMER FOR 4 MINUTES**
6. Reduce heat to MEDIUM after 1 minute (prevents burning)
7. At 4 minutes, peek under edge - should be golden brown

### Step 5: Flip and Finish (3-5 minutes)
1. Flip chops with tongs (should release easily)
2. Add 2 tbsp (28g) butter, garlic cloves, thyme to pan
3. **SET TIMER FOR 3 MINUTES**
4. Tilt pan, baste chops with butter
5. Check temp at 3 minutes:
   - Insert thermometer through SIDE into center
   - Avoid bone (reads hotter)
   - Looking for 140°F (60°C)
6. If not there, continue 1-2 more minutes
7. 🚨 **PULL AT 140°F** - will rise to 145°F during rest

### Step 6: Rest (MANDATORY - 5 minutes minimum)
1. When internal temp hits 140°F (60°C), remove from pan
2. Transfer to plate
3. Tent loosely with foil
4. **SET TIMER FOR 5 MINUTES** (3 minutes minimum for safety)
5. Temp will rise to 145°F (63°C)
6. Juices redistribute

### Step 7: Serve
1. Check final temp: Should be 145°F (63°C)
2. Should have slight pink center (this is SAFE and correct)
3. Spoon pan butter/garlic over chops
4. Serve immediately

## Cooking Times by Thickness

| Thickness | Side 1 | Side 2 | Total Time |
|-----------|--------|--------|------------|
| 3/4" (2cm) | 3-4 min | 2-3 min | 5-7 min |
| 1" (2.5cm) | 4-5 min | 3-4 min | 7-9 min |
| 1.5" (3.8cm) | 5-6 min | 4-5 min | 9-11 min |

**Always verify with thermometer**

## Perfect Pork Chop Indicators
- Internal temp: 145°F (63°C) after resting
- Slight pink center (SAFE!)
- Golden-brown crust both sides
- Juicy, not dry
- Tender texture

## Troubleshooting

**Dry pork chops:**
- Cooked past 145°F (pull at 140°F)
- Didn't brine
- Cooked too long
- Used too-lean chops

**Not golden brown:**
- Pan not hot enough
- Chops too wet (didn't pat dry)
- Moved before crust formed

**Still pink - is it safe?:**
- YES! 145°F with 3-min rest is USDA safe since 2011
- Pink = juicy and correct
- Gray throughout = overcooked

**Tough/rubbery:**
- Overcooked (past 150°F)
- Didn't rest
- Chops too thin (get thicker ones)

## Chop Selection

**Best:**
- **Bone-in rib chops**: Most flavorful, juicy (like ribeye)
- **Bone-in center-cut**: Good balance
- **Thick chops**: 1-1.5" (easier to cook right)

**OK:**
- Boneless (less forgiving, easier to overcook)

**Avoid:**
- Thin chops (under 3/4") - overcook instantly
- "Enhanced" pork (pre-brined with solution)

## Quick Pan Sauce (2 minutes)
After removing chops:
1. Add 1/4 cup (60ml) white wine or cider to hot pan
2. Scrape up brown bits
3. Add 1/2 cup (120ml) chicken stock
4. Add 1 tbsp (15g) Dijon mustard
5. Simmer, reduce by half
6. Mount with 1 tbsp (14g) butter
7. Add 1 tbsp (15ml) apple cider vinegar
8. Spoon over chops

## Pro Tips
- **Buy heritage breeds** if available (Berkshire, Duroc) - more marbling, flavor
- **Bone-in >>> boneless** (bone adds flavor, moisture buffer)
- **Look for marbling** (white fat streaks in meat)
- **Dry brine option**: Salt chops, refrigerate uncovered 1-24 hours
- **Apple/pork pairing** is classic for reason (acidity balances richness)
- **Sage and thyme** are traditional pork herbs
- **Can stuff thick chops**: Cut pocket, stuff with cheese/herbs
- **Save bones** for pork stock

## Old vs New Guidelines

**OLD (pre-2011):**
- USDA recommended 160°F (71°C)
- Result: Dry, gray, tough pork
- Based on outdated trichinosis fears

**NEW (2011-present):**
- USDA safe temp: 145°F (63°C) with 3-minute rest
- Result: Juicy, slightly pink, tender
- Trichinosis virtually eliminated in US pork
- Same safety, better results
