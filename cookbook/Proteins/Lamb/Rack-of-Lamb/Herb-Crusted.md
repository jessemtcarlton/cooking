# Herb-Crusted Rack of Lamb

## Overview
The ultimate special-occasion protein. Expensive and impressive - cannot afford to mess up. This method guarantees perfect medium-rare (130-135°F) with crispy herb crust.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **EXPENSIVE**: $25-40 per rack - use thermometer, no guessing
- **SEAR FIRST, CRUST LAST**: Herb crust burns if on from start
- **PULL AT 125°F**: Rests to 130-135°F perfect medium-rare
- **SIZE MATTERS**: 8-bone rack serves 2-3 (4 chops per person ideal)
- **REST 10 MINUTES**: Carryover cooking critical, juices redistribute
- **FRENCHED IS BEST**: Clean bones (butcher can do this)

## Target Temperature
- **Pull at**: 125°F (52°C) for medium-rare
- **After resting**: 130-135°F (54-57°C) - perfect pink
- **Medium**: 135-140°F (too much for lamb)
- **Well-done**: Don't do this to expensive lamb

## Equipment
- Heavy oven-safe skillet (cast iron ideal)
- Instant-read thermometer (MANDATORY)
- Food processor or knife
- Pastry brush
- Timer

## Ingredients (serves 4)
**For Lamb:**
- 2 racks of lamb (8 bones each, about 1.5-2 lbs each, frenched)
- 2 tbsp (30ml) neutral oil
- 1 tsp (5g) kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp (2g) black pepper

**For Herb Crust:**
- 1 cup (40g) fresh bread crumbs (panko works)
- 1/2 cup (20g) fresh parsley, chopped
- 2 tbsp (10g) fresh rosemary, chopped
- 2 tbsp (10g) fresh thyme leaves
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 3 tbsp (45ml) olive oil
- 1 tsp (3g) Dijon mustard
- Zest of 1 lemon
- 1/2 tsp (3g) kosher salt
- 1/4 tsp (1g) black pepper

**For Mustard Glue:**
- 2 tbsp (30g) Dijon mustard

## Lamb Selection

**What to Buy:**
- **Frenched rack**: Bones cleaned (prettier, easier to eat)
- **Domestic or New Zealand**: Both good, NZ is smaller/leaner
- **8-bone rack**: Standard size (serves 2-3)
- **Look for**: Pink meat, white fat, frenched bones

**Ask Butcher:**
- "I need 2 frenched racks of lamb"
- "Please remove silver skin and excess fat cap"
- "Leave about 1/4" fat cap"

## Method

### Step 1: Prepare Herb Crust (can do hours ahead)
1. In food processor, combine:
   - 1 cup (40g) bread crumbs
   - 1/2 cup (20g) parsley
   - 2 tbsp (10g) rosemary
   - 2 tbsp (10g) thyme
   - 4 cloves garlic
   - Lemon zest
   - 1/2 tsp (3g) salt
   - 1/4 tsp (1g) pepper
2. Pulse until finely chopped
3. Add 3 tbsp (45ml) olive oil + 1 tsp (3g) Dijon
4. Pulse to combine (should be like wet sand)
5. Set aside (can refrigerate up to 1 day)

### Step 2: Prep and Season Lamb (30 minutes before)
1. Remove lamb from fridge
2. Let sit at room temp 30 minutes
3. Pat DRY with paper towels
4. Season both sides:
   - 1/2 tsp (2.5g) salt per rack
   - Black pepper
5. 🚨 **Check for silver skin** (thin membrane):
   - If present, remove with sharp knife
   - Slides under membrane, peel off
   - Tough if left on

### Step 3: Preheat Oven
1. Preheat to 400°F (204°C)
2. **SET TIMER FOR 15 MINUTES** - must be fully preheated

### Step 4: Sear the Lamb (4-5 minutes total)
1. Heat oven-safe skillet over HIGH heat (2 minutes)
2. Add 2 tbsp (30ml) oil - should shimmer/smoke
3. 🚨 **Turn on exhaust fan** - will smoke
4. Place racks meat-side DOWN (not bone-side)
5. Sear 2-3 minutes (don't move)
6. Should develop deep brown crust
7. Using tongs, sear fat cap side 1-2 minutes
8. Sear ends (30 seconds each)
9. 🚨 **DON'T sear bone side** - unnecessary
10. Remove from heat

### Step 5: Apply Mustard and Herb Crust
1. Let lamb cool 2 minutes (so crust doesn't burn)
2. Brush 2 tbsp (30g) Dijon all over meat side (not bones)
3. Press herb crust onto mustard-coated meat
4. Pack firmly (should stick well)
5. Wrap bone tips with foil (prevents burning)

### Step 6: Roast (12-18 minutes)
1. Place skillet in 400°F (204°C) oven
2. **SET TIMER FOR 12 MINUTES** (for medium-rare)
3. At 12 minutes, check internal temp:
   - Insert thermometer into center (thickest part)
   - Avoid bone (reads hotter)
   - Looking for 120-125°F (49-52°C)
4. 🚨 **TIMING BY TEMP, NOT TIME:**
   - At 120°F: Pull now (will reach 125-130°F rare/medium-rare)
   - At 125°F: Pull now (will reach 130-135°F medium-rare) ⭐ PERFECT
   - At 130°F: Pull now (will reach 135-140°F medium)
   - Not there: Check every 2 minutes

**Time Estimates (for 125°F pull temp):**
- Small rack (1.5 lbs): 10-12 minutes
- Medium rack (1.75 lbs): 12-15 minutes
- Large rack (2 lbs): 15-18 minutes

### Step 7: Rest (MANDATORY - 10 minutes)
1. Remove from oven when temp reaches 125°F (52°C)
2. Transfer to cutting board
3. Tent loosely with foil
4. **SET TIMER FOR 10 MINUTES**
5. 🚨 **MUST REST**:
   - Temp rises to 130-135°F (perfect)
   - Juices redistribute
   - Allows carving without juice loss

### Step 8: Carve and Serve
1. Remove foil from bones
2. Cut between bones to create chops
3. Each person gets 3-4 chops (double or single rib)
4. Arrange on plates, bones up (dramatic presentation)
5. Drizzle with any accumulated juices
6. Optional: Serve with mint sauce or red wine reduction

## Perfect Rack of Lamb Indicators
- Internal temp: 130-135°F (54-57°C) after resting
- Medium-rare throughout (warm pink center)
- Golden herb crust (not burned)
- Juicy, tender meat
- Fat cap rendered and crispy

## Carving Options

**Individual Chops:**
- Cut between each bone
- 8 chops per rack
- Serve 3-4 per person

**Double Chops:**
- Cut every 2 bones
- More impressive presentation
- 4 double chops per rack

**Presentation:**
- Arrange bones-up (looks elegant)
- Cross bones over each other
- Garnish with rosemary sprig

## Troubleshooting

**Overcooked (gray throughout):**
- Cooked past 135°F
- Didn't use thermometer
- Lamb was too cold when started
- **Prevention**: Pull at 125°F, use thermometer

**Undercooked (too rare):**
- Pulled before 120°F
- Didn't account for resting rise
- **Fix**: Slice, return to 400°F oven for 2-3 minutes

**Burned herb crust:**
- Applied before searing (herbs burn)
- Oven too hot (reduce to 375°F)
- Cooked too long

**Tough/Chewy:**
- Overcooked (past medium)
- Didn't remove silver skin
- Old lamb (ask for fresh)

**Not crispy crust:**
- Herb mixture too wet (reduce oil)
- Oven not hot enough
- Didn't sear first

## Timing for Dinner Party

**2 hours before:**
- Make herb crust, refrigerate
- Remove lamb from fridge

**30 minutes before:**
- Preheat oven
- Season lamb

**20 minutes before:**
- Sear lamb (5 min)
- Apply crust
- Roast (12-15 min)

**10 minutes before:**
- Remove from oven
- Rest

**Serve time:**
- Carve and plate

## Variations

### Pistachio-Crusted
- Replace breadcrumbs with ground pistachios
- Add 1 tbsp honey to crust

### Mustard-Crusted (No Herbs)
- Coat with thick Dijon, no breadcrumbs
- Simpler, lets lamb flavor shine

### Mediterranean
- Add oregano, mint to herb crust
- Include lemon zest, pine nuts

### Reverse-Sear Method (More Forgiving)
- Roast at 275°F (135°C) until 115°F internal
- Sear in hot pan 2 min per side
- Apply crust, broil 2 minutes
- More control, harder to overcook

## Pro Tips
- **Ask butcher to french** - saves time, they do it better
- **Room temp meat** cooks more evenly
- **Mustard isn't just glue** - adds tangy flavor
- **Cover bone tips** with foil or they burn
- **Use metal skillet** that can go oven→stove
- **Save bones** for lamb stock
- **Domestic vs NZ lamb**: Domestic is larger, fattier; NZ is leaner, smaller
- **Chine bone removed**: Makes carving easier (ask butcher)
- **Can prep day ahead**: Sear and crust, refrigerate, roast next day

## Cost Savings
- **Buy whole lamb loin**, french yourself (saves $5-10)
- **Shop sales** (rack of lamb often on sale around holidays)
- **Buy from butcher** not grocery (often better price and quality)
- **Costco** carries frenched racks at good price

## Wine Pairing
- **Bold reds**: Cabernet Sauvignon, Bordeaux, Syrah
- **Why**: Lamb's rich flavor needs bold wine
- **Avoid**: White wine (doesn't match richness)

## Special Occasion Tips
- This is **THE** special occasion protein
- Perfect for: Anniversaries, holidays, impressing in-laws
- Looks and tastes like $100 restaurant dish
