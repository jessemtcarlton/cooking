# Béchamel Sauce (White Sauce)

## Overview
The foundation of cream sauces, cheese sauces, and gratins. Master this and you can make mac and cheese, lasagna, croque monsieur, and hundreds of derivatives.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **ROUX MUST COOK**: Raw flour tastes terrible - cook 2-3 minutes minimum
- **ADD MILK SLOWLY**: Too fast = lumps that won't dissolve
- **MILK MUST BE WARM**: Cold milk + hot roux = instant lumps
- **CONSTANT WHISKING**: Stop whisking = scorched bottom
- **SIMMER TO THICKEN**: Won't fully thicken until simmered 10+ minutes

## Ratios by Thickness

**Thin Béchamel** (for soups):
- 2 tbsp (28g) butter
- 2 tbsp (16g) flour
- 2 cups (480ml) milk

**Medium Béchamel** (standard, most uses):
- 3 tbsp (42g) butter
- 3 tbsp (24g) flour
- 2 cups (480ml) milk

**Thick Béchamel** (for soufflés, croquettes):
- 4 tbsp (56g) butter
- 4 tbsp (32g) flour
- 2 cups (480ml) milk

## Ingredients (Medium Thickness, makes 2 cups)
- 3 tbsp (42g) unsalted butter
- 3 tbsp (24g) all-purpose flour
- 2 cups (480ml) whole milk (warm)
- 1/4 tsp (1.5g) kosher salt
- 1/8 tsp white pepper
- Pinch of nutmeg (freshly grated)
- Optional: 1 bay leaf, 1 small onion studded with 2 cloves

## Equipment
- Medium saucepan (2-3 quart)
- Whisk
- Wooden spoon
- Measuring cups/spoons
- Fine mesh strainer (optional)

## Method

### Step 1: Warm the Milk (5 minutes before)
1. Heat 2 cups (480ml) milk in separate saucepan or microwave
2. Warm to about 120-140°F (49-60°C) - steaming but not boiling
3. 🚨 **WARM MILK = NO LUMPS**
4. Keep warm while making roux

**Traditional Method (Optional):**
- Add 1 bay leaf + onion studded with 2 cloves to cold milk
- Heat gently to infuse (10 minutes)
- Strain before using

### Step 2: Make White Roux (3-4 minutes)
1. Melt 3 tbsp (42g) butter in saucepan over MEDIUM heat
2. When butter is melted and foamy, add 3 tbsp (24g) flour
3. Whisk immediately to combine
4. Cook, whisking constantly, for 2-3 minutes
5. Roux should be foamy, smell nutty (NOT floury)
6. Should remain pale - NO browning
7. 🚨 **MUST COOK FULL 2-3 MINUTES** or sauce tastes raw

### Step 3: Add Milk Gradually (5-7 minutes)
1. Remove roux from heat temporarily
2. Add about 1/4 cup (60ml) warm milk
3. Whisk vigorously - will be very thick paste
4. Return to MEDIUM heat
5. Continue adding milk in small additions:
   - 1/4 cup at a time
   - Whisk until smooth after each addition
   - Sauce will thin as you add more milk
6. Once half the milk is added, can add faster (1/2 cup at a time)
7. 🚨 **WHISK CONSTANTLY** - prevents lumps and scorching

### Step 4: Simmer and Thicken (10-15 minutes)
1. Once all milk is incorporated, increase heat to MEDIUM-HIGH
2. Bring to gentle simmer (bubbles breaking surface)
3. Reduce heat to MEDIUM-LOW
4. Simmer gently, whisking frequently, 10-15 minutes
5. 🚨 **MUST SIMMER** - raw starch taste cooks out, sauce thickens fully
6. Sauce is ready when:
   - Coats back of spoon
   - When you run finger through coating, line stays clear
   - Smooth, glossy, no graininess
   - Consistency of heavy cream (medium thickness)

### Step 5: Season (final step)
1. Remove from heat
2. Add 1/4 tsp (1.5g) salt
3. Add 1/8 tsp white pepper
4. Add pinch freshly grated nutmeg
5. Taste and adjust seasoning
6. 🚨 **Season at end** - sauce reduces while cooking, concentrates salt

### Step 6: Strain (optional but professional)
1. Pour through fine mesh strainer
2. Removes any lumps or skin
3. Press through with spatula
4. Results in silky-smooth sauce

## Perfect Béchamel Indicators
- Smooth, no lumps
- Glossy, ivory color
- Coats back of spoon (nappe consistency)
- Pourable but thick
- No raw flour taste
- Subtle nutmeg aroma

## Holding and Storing

**To Hold (1-2 hours):**
- Press plastic wrap directly on surface (prevents skin)
- Keep warm over low heat
- Whisk occasionally
- Add splash of milk if too thick

**To Store:**
- Refrigerate up to 3 days in airtight container
- Press plastic wrap on surface
- Reheat gently over low heat
- Whisk in milk to restore consistency
- May need to strain if lumpy after reheating

## Common Derivatives

### Mornay Sauce (Cheese Sauce)
**For mac and cheese, vegetables gratin:**
1. Make béchamel as above
2. Remove from heat
3. Add 1 cup (100g) grated Gruyère cheese
4. Add 1/4 cup (25g) grated Parmesan
5. Whisk until melted and smooth
6. Don't return to heat (cheese can break)

**Uses:** Mac and cheese, cauliflower gratin, croque monsieur

### Sauce Soubise (Onion Sauce)
**For roasted meats, vegetables:**
1. Make béchamel as above
2. Cook 1 large onion (150g), finely diced, in butter until very soft
3. Purée onion until smooth
4. Fold 1 cup onion purée into béchamel
5. Strain for ultra-smooth sauce

**Uses:** Roast pork, veal, eggs

### Cream Sauce (Sauce Crème)
**For fish, chicken, vegetables:**
1. Make béchamel but use only 1.5 cups (360ml) milk
2. Add 1/2 cup (120ml) heavy cream at end
3. Richer than standard béchamel

**Uses:** Fish, chicken, pasta

### Mustard Sauce
**For pork, ham, fish:**
1. Make béchamel as above
2. Whisk in 2 tbsp (30g) Dijon mustard at end
3. Add 1 tsp (5ml) lemon juice

**Uses:** Pork chops, ham, salmon

## Troubleshooting

**Lumpy sauce:**
- Milk was too cold
- Added milk too fast
- Didn't whisk enough
- **Fix:** Strain through fine mesh strainer OR blend with immersion blender

**Too thick:**
- Too much flour or over-reduced
- **Fix:** Whisk in warm milk, 2 tbsp at a time, until desired consistency

**Too thin:**
- Not enough roux or didn't simmer long enough
- **Fix:** Simmer longer (up to 20 minutes) OR make small roux separately, whisk in

**Tastes floury/raw:**
- Didn't cook roux long enough OR didn't simmer sauce long enough
- **Fix:** Simmer 10 more minutes - should cook out

**Scorched/burned bottom:**
- Heat too high or didn't whisk enough
- **Fix:** Can't fix burned flavor - start over. Prevent by constant whisking, medium heat

**Skin formed on top:**
- Left uncovered while holding
- **Fix:** Whisk skin in OR strain it out. Prevent with plastic wrap on surface

## Uses for Béchamel

**Base for:**
- Mac and cheese
- Lasagna
- Moussaka
- Croque monsieur/madame
- Soufflés (thick béchamel)
- Croquettes
- Gratins (vegetables)
- Creamed spinach
- Creamed corn

**Pairing:**
- Any dish needing creamy, mild sauce
- Balances acidic ingredients (tomatoes)
- Binds casseroles and baked pasta

## Pro Tips
- Use a flat whisk (roux whisk) for corners of pan
- If making large batch, make roux first, let cool, store (add milk later)
- Can make with 2% milk (less rich) or half-and-half (richer)
- Nutmeg is traditional but optional
- Fresh nutmeg >>> pre-ground (night and day difference)
- Scale recipe: Ratio is always 1.5 tbsp butter + 1.5 tbsp flour per 1 cup milk (medium)
- For dairy-free: Use oat milk or soy milk (best texture)
