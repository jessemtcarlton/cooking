# Chicken Stock (From Scratch)

## Overview
Foundation of soups, sauces, risotto, and braises. Homemade stock is incomparably better than store-bought. Uses bones, aromatics, and time to extract maximum flavor and gelatin.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **NEVER BOIL**: Boiling = cloudy, greasy stock - GENTLE simmer only
- **SKIM RELIGIOUSLY**: First 30 minutes critical - skim every 10 minutes
- **TIME MATTERS**: 3-4 hours minimum - shortcuts = weak stock
- **DON'T SALT**: Add salt when using stock, not during making
- **COOL SAFELY**: Large pot of hot stock = food safety risk if cooled wrong

## Ratios
- **Bones**: 3-4 lbs (1.4-1.8kg) per gallon (3.8L) water
- **Mirepoix**: 1 lb (450g) total per gallon
- **Water**: Cover bones by 2 inches (5cm)
- **Time**: Simmer 3-4 hours for chicken, 4-6 hours for beef

## Ingredients (makes 3-4 quarts)
- 4 lbs (1.8kg) chicken bones (backs, necks, wings, carcass)
- 1 gallon (3.8L) cold water
- 2 cups (300g) onions, roughly chopped (about 2 medium)
- 1 cup (150g) carrots, roughly chopped (about 2 medium)
- 1 cup (150g) celery, roughly chopped (about 3 stalks)
- 4-5 cloves garlic, smashed
- 2 bay leaves
- 6-8 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1 tsp dried)
- 6-8 sprigs fresh parsley (stems OK)
- 1 tsp (4g) black peppercorns
- Optional: 1 Parmesan rind, 2 tomatoes (adds umami)

## Equipment
- Large stock pot (8-12 quart)
- Ladle
- Fine mesh strainer or chinois
- Cheesecloth (for crystal-clear stock)
- Large bowl or containers for storing
- Fat separator (optional but helpful)

## Method

### Step 1: Prepare Bones (Optional - for darker stock)
**For Roasted Chicken Stock** (richer, deeper flavor):
1. Preheat oven to 425°F (220°C)
2. Spread bones on baking sheet
3. Roast 45 minutes, turning once
4. Bones should be deep golden brown
5. Use roasted bones for stock
6. Deglaze roasting pan with 1 cup water, add to stock

**For White Chicken Stock** (lighter, more delicate):
- Use raw bones, skip roasting

### Step 2: Start the Stock (First 30 minutes)
1. Place 4 lbs (1.8kg) bones in stock pot
2. Cover with 1 gallon (3.8L) COLD water
3. Water should cover bones by 2" (5cm)
4. 🚨 **START WITH COLD WATER** - hot water = cloudy stock
5. Bring to bare simmer over MEDIUM-HIGH heat
6. Takes 20-30 minutes to heat

**🚨 CRITICAL FIRST 30 MINUTES:**
7. As stock heats, foam/scum rises to surface
8. Skim every 5-10 minutes with ladle
9. Remove all gray/brown foam
10. This is impurities/proteins - removing = clear stock
11. Keep skimming until foam is minimal (20-30 min)

### Step 3: Add Aromatics (after skimming)
1. Once foam is mostly gone (30 min in), add vegetables:
   - 2 cups (300g) onions, roughly chopped
   - 1 cup (150g) carrots, roughly chopped
   - 1 cup (150g) celery, roughly chopped
   - 4-5 cloves garlic, smashed
2. Add herbs and spices:
   - 2 bay leaves
   - 6-8 sprigs thyme
   - 6-8 sprigs parsley
   - 1 tsp (4g) peppercorns
3. 🚨 **DO NOT ADD SALT**
4. Stir gently to submerge

### Step 4: Simmer (3-4 hours)
1. Reduce heat to LOW
2. Stock should barely simmer:
   - **Lazy bubbles breaking surface**
   - **NOT rolling boil** (makes cloudy, greasy)
   - Temperature around 180-190°F (82-88°C)
3. **SET TIMER FOR 3 HOURS**
4. Leave uncovered or partially covered
5. Check every 30-60 minutes:
   - Maintain gentle simmer
   - Skim any new foam/fat
   - Add water if level drops below bones

**Timing Guide:**
- **Minimum**: 3 hours (good stock)
- **Optimal**: 4 hours (great stock)
- **Maximum**: 6 hours (diminishing returns, can get bitter)

### Step 5: Strain the Stock (careful - it's hot!)
1. Remove from heat
2. Let rest 10 minutes (solids settle)
3. Set fine mesh strainer over large bowl/pot
4. Carefully ladle stock through strainer
   - Don't pour (keeps sediment out)
5. Discard solids (bones, vegetables)
6. 🚨 **Optional for crystal-clear stock:**
   - Line strainer with cheesecloth
   - Strain again through cheesecloth
   - Don't press solids (makes cloudy)

### Step 6: Defat the Stock
**Immediate Use Method:**
1. Use fat separator
2. Pour stock in, fat rises
3. Pour from bottom spout (fat stays in top)

**Make-Ahead Method** (BEST):
1. Refrigerate stock overnight
2. Fat solidifies on top
3. Lift off and discard solid fat layer
4. Easier and cleaner than hot defatting

### Step 7: Cool and Store (FOOD SAFETY CRITICAL)
**🚨 Danger Zone: 40-140°F (4-60°C)**
Stock is high-risk for bacteria. Must cool quickly.

**Safe Cooling Methods:**

**Ice Bath (Best for same-day use):**
1. Fill sink or large container with ice water
2. Place stock pot in ice bath
3. Stir occasionally
4. Cool to room temp (1-2 hours)
5. Refrigerate immediately

**Smaller Containers (Fastest):**
1. Divide into smaller containers (2-4 cup portions)
2. Cools faster than one large pot
3. Refrigerate uncovered until cool
4. Cover once cold

**DON'T:**
- Leave on counter overnight (DANGEROUS)
- Put hot stock directly in fridge (warms fridge, makes other food unsafe)

## Storage

**Refrigerator:**
- 4-5 days in airtight container
- Fat layer on top acts as seal (remove before using)
- Should gel when cold (sign of good collagen extraction)

**Freezer:**
- 4-6 months
- Freeze in portions:
  - Ice cube trays (2 tbsp portions)
  - 1 cup containers
  - 2 cup containers
  - 4 cup containers
- Label with date
- Remove air to prevent freezer burn

## Quality Indicators

**Perfect Stock:**
- **Color**: Deep golden yellow (white) or amber (roasted)
- **Clarity**: Clear, not cloudy
- **Aroma**: Rich, chickeny, aromatic
- **Body**: Gels when cold (high gelatin from bones)
- **Taste**: Rich, savory, balanced (not salty)

**Good Stock Gels:**
- When refrigerated, should be jiggly like Jell-O
- Sign of collagen from bones (creates silky mouthfeel in sauces)
- If doesn't gel: Still usable but less body
- Why doesn't gel: Not enough bones, not simmered long enough, or old bones

## Variations

### Brown Chicken Stock
- Roast bones at 425°F (220°C) for 45 min before using
- Roast mirepoix vegetables too (30 min)
- Richer, darker, more intense

### White Chicken Stock (Classic French)
- Use raw bones
- Don't brown anything
- Lighter, more delicate
- Better for delicate sauces

### Chinese-Style Stock
- Add 3-4 slices fresh ginger
- Add 2 scallions
- Add 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine at end
- Omit thyme, reduce peppercorns

### Asian-Inspired
- Add lemongrass, ginger, star anise
- Add fish sauce at end (1-2 tbsp)
- Omit Western herbs

## Troubleshooting

**Stock is cloudy:**
- Boiled too hard (must be gentle simmer)
- Didn't skim enough in first 30 min
- Stirred too much (agitated sediment)
- Poured instead of ladling when straining
- **Fix:** Can clarify with egg white raft (advanced) or accept it

**Stock is greasy:**
- Didn't defat properly
- Boiled too hard (extracted too much fat)
- **Fix:** Refrigerate, remove fat cap, or use fat separator

**Stock is weak/watery:**
- Not enough bones (need 3-4 lbs per gallon)
- Didn't simmer long enough (need 3+ hours)
- Too much water
- **Fix:** Reduce by simmering to concentrate (don't add salt until done)

**Stock is bitter:**
- Simmered too long (over 6 hours can get bitter)
- Vegetables too burnt (if roasting)
- **Fix:** Can't fix, start over

**Stock doesn't gel:**
- Not enough bones with joints/cartilage
- Didn't simmer long enough
- Bones were too lean (breast meat bones have less collagen)
- **Still usable** - just has less body

**Stock is too dark:**
- Roasted bones too long or too high
- Vegetables burnt
- **Still usable** if tastes OK

## Best Bones for Stock

**Best (highest gelatin):**
- Chicken backs
- Chicken necks
- Chicken feet (maximum gelatin)
- Wing tips
- Carcasses from roasted chicken

**Good:**
- Whole chicken wings
- Chicken frames (after butchering)

**OK:**
- Chicken thighs (with bones)
- Drumsticks

**Not Ideal:**
- Chicken breasts (lean, less flavor, expensive)

## Cost Savings

**Get Bones Free/Cheap:**
- Ask butcher for backs/necks (often free)
- Save bones from rotisserie chicken
- Buy whole chickens, butcher, freeze bones
- Buy chicken feet from Asian markets (cheap, maximum gelatin)
- Collect bones in freezer bag until you have enough

**Stock Economics:**
- Homemade: ~$0.50-1.00 per quart
- Store-bought (good quality): $3-5 per quart
- Make 4 quarts = save $12-20

## Remouillage (Second Stock)

**Get more from bones:**
1. After straining first stock, save bones
2. Cover bones with fresh water again
3. Simmer 2-3 hours
4. Second stock is weaker but still useful
5. Use for:
   - Cooking rice/grains
   - Braising liquid (reinforced with first stock)
   - Freezing for future stock (concentrated by reduction)

## Pro Tips
- Add Parmesan rind (free from cheese shops) for umami
- Add dried mushrooms (porcini) for extra depth
- Don't add too many vegetables (overwhelming) - ratio matters
- Freeze stock in ice cube trays (perfect for deglazing single servings)
- Reduce stock by half = glace (concentrated, freeze in ice cubes)
- Use a mix of bones and meat scraps (meat = flavor, bones = body)
- Blanch bones first (boil 5 min, rinse) for clearest stock
- Save vegetable scraps (onion skins, carrot peels, celery tops) in freezer for stock
