# Classic Parmesan Risotto (Risotto alla Parmigiana)

## Overview
The ultimate test of technique. Creamy, flowing risotto with perfectly cooked rice - not mushy, not crunchy. Requires constant attention for 20-25 minutes but worth every minute.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **CONSTANT STIRRING**: Stop stirring = stuck rice, uneven cooking
- **STOCK MUST BE HOT**: Cold stock = temperature shock, stops cooking
- **ADD LIQUID GRADUALLY**: Too much at once = mushy rice
- **FINISH OFF HEAT**: Cheese added on heat = grainy, broken
- **SERVE IMMEDIATELY**: Risotto waits for no one - continues thickening
- **TIMING IS EVERYTHING**: 18-22 minutes from first ladle - not faster

## Ratios (Critical for Success)
- **Rice to liquid**: 1 cup (200g) rice : 3-4 cups (720-960ml) stock
- **Final ratio**: Should be loose, flowing (all'onda - "like a wave")
- **Cheese**: 1/2-3/4 cup (50-75g) per 1 cup rice

## Equipment
- Heavy-bottomed pot or sauté pan (3-4 quart)
- Separate saucepan for stock
- Ladle
- Wooden spoon
- Timer
- Measuring cups

## Ingredients (serves 4 as side, 2-3 as main)
- 1.5 cups (300g) Arborio, Carnaroli, or Vialone Nano rice
- 6 cups (1.4L) chicken stock (homemade best)
- 3 tbsp (42g) butter, divided
- 2 tbsp (30ml) olive oil
- 1 small onion (100g), finely minced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/2 cup (120ml) dry white wine
- 3/4 cup (75g) Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
- Salt and white pepper to taste
- Optional: 2 tbsp (30ml) heavy cream (for extra richness)

## Rice Selection

**Best Risotto Rice:**
1. **Carnaroli** - "Caviar of rice," best quality, most forgiving
2. **Arborio** - Most common, readily available, good
3. **Vialone Nano** - Traditional Venetian, smaller grain

**🚨 DO NOT USE:**
- Long grain rice (jasmine, basmati)
- Medium grain rice
- Instant rice
- Only short-grain Italian rice works (high starch = creamy)

## Method

### Step 1: Heat the Stock (before starting)
1. Pour 6 cups (1.4L) stock in saucepan
2. Bring to simmer over medium heat
3. Reduce to LOW, keep HOT (not boiling)
4. 🚨 **MUST STAY HOT** - cold stock stops cooking, makes gummy rice
5. Keep at gentle simmer throughout

### Step 2: Toast the Rice (4-5 minutes)
1. Heat large pot over MEDIUM heat
2. Add 2 tbsp (28g) butter + 2 tbsp (30ml) olive oil
3. When butter melted, add minced onion
4. Cook 3-4 minutes until soft (don't brown)
5. Add garlic, cook 30 seconds
6. Add 1.5 cups (300g) rice
7. Stir to coat every grain with fat
8. Cook 2-3 minutes, stirring constantly
9. Rice should become translucent around edges
10. Should smell nutty, toasted
11. 🚨 **DON'T SKIP TOASTING** - develops flavor, helps rice hold shape

### Step 3: Deglaze with Wine (2-3 minutes)
1. Add 1/2 cup (120ml) white wine
2. Stir constantly
3. Wine will sizzle and steam
4. Cook until wine almost completely absorbed
5. Should smell aromatic, no raw alcohol smell
6. **SET TIMER FOR 18 MINUTES** - risotto cooking time starts NOW

### Step 4: Add Stock Gradually (18-22 minutes - CRITICAL)
**This is the critical part - timing and technique matter:**

1. Add 1 ladle (about 3/4 cup/180ml) hot stock
2. Stir constantly in figure-8 pattern
3. Cook until stock almost absorbed (rice just barely covered)
4. Add another ladle
5. Stir constantly
6. Repeat this process:
   - **Add ladle of stock**
   - **Stir constantly until absorbed**
   - **Add another ladle**
   - **Repeat**

**🚨 CRITICAL STIRRING TECHNIQUE:**
- Must stir constantly (every 10-20 seconds minimum)
- Figure-8 pattern through rice
- Scrape bottom and sides
- Releases starch = creates creaminess
- Prevents sticking and burning
- Can rest arm occasionally but never stop for more than 30 seconds

**Timing Guide:**
- Minute 0-5: Rapid absorption, ladle every 2-3 minutes
- Minute 5-15: Steady absorption, ladle every 3-4 minutes
- Minute 15-20: Slow absorption, ladle every 4-5 minutes
- Total: About 5-6 ladles of stock

### Step 5: Test for Doneness (18-22 minutes)
1. At 18 minutes, taste a grain of rice
2. Should be:
   - **Al dente**: Tender but with slight firmness in center
   - **NOT mushy**: Grains distinct, not paste
   - **NOT crunchy**: No hard white center
   - **Creamy**: Rice releases starch, creates creamy consistency
3. If still crunchy: Continue adding stock, cooking, tasting every 2 minutes
4. 🚨 **TIMING**:
   - Minimum: 18 minutes (rare)
   - Optimal: 20-22 minutes (most common)
   - Maximum: 25 minutes (if rice is old)

### Step 6: Finish with Cheese and Butter (OFF HEAT)
**🚨 CRITICAL: Remove from heat first**

1. Remove pot from heat
2. Add 1 tbsp (14g) butter (remaining butter)
3. Add 3/4 cup (75g) grated Parmesan
4. Optional: Add 2 tbsp (30ml) heavy cream
5. Stir vigorously 30 seconds
6. Risotto should be loose, flowing
7. **All'onda test**: Spoon risotto onto plate - should slowly spread out in waves
8. If too thick: Add splash of hot stock
9. Season with salt and white pepper to taste

### Step 7: Serve IMMEDIATELY
1. Spoon into warm bowls
2. Top with extra Parmesan
3. Optional: Drizzle olive oil, black pepper
4. **Serve within 2 minutes**
5. 🚨 **Risotto keeps cooking** - gets thick/gummy if sits

## Perfect Risotto Indicators

**Texture:**
- **All'onda**: "Like a wave" - flows slowly when plated
- **Creamy**: Starch creates cream sauce (no actual cream added to classic)
- **Al dente rice**: Grains tender but firm
- **Not soupy**: Should flow, not pool
- **Not stiff**: Should not hold shape like pilaf

**Appearance:**
- Ivory color (Parmesan risotto)
- Glossy, not matte
- Individual grains visible
- Creamy sauce coating rice

**Taste:**
- Rich, savory
- Wine and stock flavor balanced
- Cheese prominent but not overwhelming
- Al dente texture

## Troubleshooting

**Mushy rice:**
- Overcooked (past al dente stage)
- Added too much liquid at once
- Cooked too long
- **Can't fix** - make rice pudding or arancini

**Crunchy rice:**
- Undercooked
- Not enough liquid
- Heat too high (liquid evaporated too fast)
- **Fix**: Add more hot stock, continue cooking

**Gummy/Sticky:**
- Wrong rice (not risotto rice)
- Didn't stir enough
- Added cold stock
- **Can't really fix** once gummy

**Too thick:**
- Not enough liquid
- Cooked too long after last addition
- **Fix**: Stir in hot stock or cream to loosen

**Too thin/soupy:**
- Too much liquid
- Undercook (starch not released enough)
- **Fix**: Cook longer without adding liquid

**Tastes bland:**
- Didn't season enough
- Stock was weak (use homemade)
- Not enough cheese
- Didn't toast rice long enough

**Grainy cheese:**
- Added cheese on heat (protein seizes up)
- Cheese too hot
- **Always remove from heat** before adding cheese

## Timing for Dinner Party

**Cannot make ahead** - must make and serve immediately

**Timeline:**
- 30 min before: Start heating stock
- 25 min before: Begin risotto
- 20-22 min: Cook risotto
- 2 min: Finish with cheese/butter
- 0 min: SERVE (risotto waits for no one)

**Holding (emergency only):**
- Can hold 5 minutes max
- Add splash of hot stock, stir before serving
- Quality degrades quickly

## Variations

### Mushroom Risotto
- Sauté 8 oz (225g) sliced mushrooms, set aside
- Make risotto as above
- Fold in mushrooms at end
- Add dried porcini to stock for extra flavor

### Asparagus Risotto
- Blanch asparagus tips, set aside
- Add chopped asparagus stems to stock (strain out)
- Fold tips into finished risotto

### Seafood Risotto
- Use fish stock instead of chicken
- Add shrimp, scallops in last 3-4 minutes
- Omit cheese (fish + cheese = controversial)
- Finish with lemon, parsley

### Saffron Risotto (Risotto Milanese)
- Add pinch of saffron threads to hot stock
- Classic accompaniment to osso buco

### Butternut Squash Risotto
- Roast diced squash, fold in at end
- Add sage instead of Parmesan (or both)

## Pro Tips

**Stock Quality Matters:**
- Homemade >>> store-bought (night and day difference)
- Use good stock (it's 50% of the dish)
- Weak stock = weak risotto

**Cheese Tips:**
- Grate fresh (pre-grated has anti-caking agents, won't melt smooth)
- Use real Parmigiano-Reggiano (not "Parmesan")
- Save Parmesan rinds for stock (adds umami)

**Stirring:**
- Constant stirring releases starch (creates creaminess)
- Can rest arm occasionally but keep stirring
- Use wooden spoon (gentle on rice)

**Temperature:**
- Stock must stay hot (keep simmering)
- Risotto should bubble gently, not violently
- Medium heat throughout

**Timing:**
- Start risotto when guests arrive (impressive to watch)
- Or make just before serving
- Cannot rush or slow down - it's ready when it's ready

**Mantecatura:**
- Final vigorous stirring off heat
- Italian term for finishing risotto
- Creates flowing, creamy consistency
- "Cream" comes from starch, not cream

## Leftovers

**Risotto doesn't reheat well** - too thick, gummy

**Use leftovers for:**
- **Arancini** (fried rice balls)
- **Risotto cakes** (form into patties, pan-fry)
- **Soup** (thin with stock, heat gently)

## Classic Italian Rules

1. **Toast rice first** (develop flavor)
2. **Add liquid gradually** (releases starch)
3. **Stir constantly** (prevents sticking, releases starch)
4. **Finish off heat** (cheese stays creamy)
5. **Serve immediately** (optimal texture moment)
6. **Al dente rice** (tender but firm)
7. **All'onda consistency** (flows like wave)
