# 🍁 Tim Hortons Honey Cruller (Copycat)

## Overview
The lightest, airiest donut in the Tim Hortons lineup. Made from choux pastry (like cream puffs), twisted into a ring, fried golden, and glazed with honey. Impossibly light texture - almost hollow inside. This copycat creates the exact crispy-outside, airy-inside texture with sweet honey glaze.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **CHOUX PASTRY METHOD**: Different from yeast donuts (cook flour first)
- **EGGS AT ROOM TEMP**: Cold eggs won't incorporate properly
- **PIPE WHILE WARM**: Dough must be warm/room temp to pipe
- **OIL TEMP 365°F**: Critical - lower = greasy, higher = burnt before cooked
- **TWIST THE RING**: Classic cruller has twisted rope shape
- **GLAZE IMMEDIATELY**: While still warm from fryer

## The Tim Hortons Secret
1. **Choux pastry** (same dough as cream puffs - creates hollow interior)
2. **Piped in twisted ring** (signature rope shape)
3. **Fried at 365°F** (puffs up, creates air pockets)
4. **Honey glaze** (simple, sweet, classic)
5. **Ultra-light texture** (mostly air - very few calories per volume)
6. **Fresh daily** (doesn't keep well - always made fresh)

## Equipment
- Large saucepan
- Wooden spoon
- Electric mixer (hand or stand)
- Piping bag with large star tip (1/2" opening)
- Parchment paper
- Scissors
- Deep fryer or heavy pot
- Oil thermometer
- Wire rack over baking sheet
- Tongs or spider strainer

## Ingredients (makes 8-10 crullers)

**For Choux Pastry:**
- 1 cup (240ml) water
- 1/2 cup (113g) butter, cut into pieces
- 1 tbsp (12g) granulated sugar
- 1/2 tsp (3g) kosher salt
- 1 cup (120g) all-purpose flour
- 4 large eggs, room temperature
- 1 tsp (5ml) vanilla extract
- Oil for frying (vegetable, about 8 cups/2L)

**For Honey Glaze:**
- 2 cups (240g) powdered sugar
- 3 tbsp (45ml) honey
- 3-4 tbsp (45-60ml) whole milk
- 1/2 tsp (2.5ml) vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

## Method

### Step 1: Make Choux Pastry (THE CRITICAL STEP)

**Cook Flour Mixture:**
1. In large saucepan, combine:
   - 1 cup (240ml) water
   - 1/2 cup (113g) butter pieces
   - 1 tbsp (12g) sugar
   - 1/2 tsp (3g) salt
2. Bring to rolling boil over medium-high heat
3. Butter should be completely melted
4. Remove from heat immediately
5. Add 1 cup (120g) flour all at once
6. Stir vigorously with wooden spoon
7. Return to medium heat
8. Stir constantly 2-3 minutes
9. Dough will form ball, leave film on pan bottom
10. Should pull away from sides of pan
11. Remove from heat
12. 🚨 **COOK UNTIL FILM FORMS** - this is critical

**Cool and Add Eggs:**
1. Transfer dough to mixing bowl
2. Let cool 5 minutes (should be warm, not hot)
3. Beat on medium-low speed
4. Add 1 egg
5. Beat until completely incorporated
6. Dough will look broken, then come together
7. Repeat with remaining 3 eggs, one at a time
8. Add 1 tsp vanilla with last egg
9. Final dough should be:
   - Smooth and glossy
   - Thick but pipeable
   - Forms soft peak when lifted
10. 🚨 **ADD EGGS ONE AT A TIME** - won't incorporate if added all at once

### Step 2: Pipe Crullers

**Prepare Parchment:**
1. Cut parchment into 4" squares (8-10 squares)
2. Lightly oil each square
3. Place on baking sheet

**Pipe Rings:**
1. Transfer warm dough to piping bag with large star tip
2. On each parchment square:
3. Pipe 3-4" circle (ring shape)
4. Overlap ends slightly
5. Twist as you pipe for rope effect
6. Should be thick (about 3/4" diameter of rope)
7. Make hole in center about 1.5"
8. 🚨 **PIPE THICK** - dough puffs but doesn't spread much

### Step 3: Chill (15-30 minutes)
1. Place baking sheet with piped crullers in fridge
2. Chill 15-30 minutes
3. Helps them hold shape when frying
4. Can chill up to 2 hours

### Step 4: Heat Oil
1. Heat oil in deep pot to 365°F (185°C)
2. Oil should be 3-4" deep
3. Use thermometer - exact temp critical
4. While oil heats, make glaze

### Step 5: Make Honey Glaze
1. In bowl, whisk together:
   - 2 cups (240g) powdered sugar
   - 3 tbsp (45ml) honey
   - 3 tbsp (45ml) milk
   - 1/2 tsp vanilla
   - Pinch salt
2. Should be thick but pourable
3. Add more milk 1 tsp at a time if too thick
4. Set aside

### Step 6: Fry Crullers (5-6 minutes each)

**The Frying Process:**
1. Oil at 365°F (185°C)
2. Pick up parchment with cruller
3. Gently flip into oil (parchment-side up)
4. Peel off parchment with tongs
5. Cruller will sink, then float
6. Fry 2-3 minutes first side
7. Should puff up significantly
8. Turn golden brown
9. Flip carefully
10. Fry 2-3 minutes second side
11. Total fry time: 5-6 minutes
12. Should be deep golden, puffed
13. Remove to wire rack
14. Fry 2 at a time max (don't crowd)
15. 🚨 **WATCH CLOSELY** - browns quickly at end

### Step 7: Glaze Immediately
1. While cruller still warm (within 2 minutes):
2. Dip top half in honey glaze
3. Let excess drip off
4. Return to wire rack glaze-side up
5. Glaze will set as cruller cools
6. 🚨 **GLAZE WHILE HOT** - won't stick if cold

### Step 8: Serve
1. Let glaze set 5-10 minutes
2. Serve at room temperature
3. Best within 4-6 hours
4. Don't store (gets soggy)

- **Glaze**: Honey-sweet, thin coating
- **Weight**: Very light (mostly air)

- Make choux pastry: 20 min
- Pipe crullers: 15 min
- Chill: 15-30 min
- Heat oil: 10 min
- Fry: 30-40 min (in batches)
- Glaze: 10 min
- **Active time: 60 min**

**Make-Ahead:**
- Pipe crullers, refrigerate up to 2 hours before frying
- Fry just before serving
- Don't make day ahead (gets soggy)

## Troubleshooting

**Crullers didn't puff:**
- Dough wasn't cooked enough (need film on pan)
- Eggs not incorporated fully
- Oil temp too low (need 365°F)
- Dough was too cold

**Dense, not airy:**
- Didn't cook flour mixture enough
- Added eggs while dough too hot
- Oil temp wrong
- Underfried

**Greasy crullers:**
- Oil temp too low
- Didn't drain on wire rack
- Overfried

**Crullers fell apart:**
- Dough too thin
- Didn't chill before frying
- Oil too hot (outside set before inside cooked)

**Glaze won't stick:**
- Cruller too cool
- Glaze too thick
- Cruller too greasy

## Variations

### Chocolate Honey Cruller
- Add 2 tbsp cocoa powder to glaze
- Reduce powdered sugar to 1 3/4 cups
- Chocolate-honey combo

### Maple Cruller
- Replace honey with maple syrup in glaze
- Add 1/2 tsp maple extract
- Very Canadian

### Cinnamon Sugar Cruller
- Skip glaze
- Toss warm crullers in cinnamon sugar
- Classic old-fashioned style

### Filled Cruller
- Make larger, thicker crullers
- After frying and cooling, fill with:
  - Vanilla custard
  - Whipped cream
  - Jam
- Like cream puff donut

## Storage

**Room Temperature:**
- Best within 4-6 hours of frying
- After that, gets soggy
- Store uncovered (covering makes soggy faster)

**Not Recommended:**
- Don't refrigerate (gets soggy immediately)
- Don't freeze (texture destroyed)
- Make fresh and eat same day

**Choux Pastry Science:**
- Cooking flour gelatinizes starch
- Creates structure that traps steam
- Eggs provide protein network
- Steam puffs up dough during frying
- Creates hollow interior

**Why So Light:**
- Choux pastry is mostly water and air
- Puffs up dramatically when fried
- Interior is hollow with thin walls
- Lowest calorie donut (by weight)
