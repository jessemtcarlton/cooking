# Red Wine Pan Sauce (Reduction)

## Overview
Classic steakhouse sauce made from pan drippings. Rich, complex, restaurant-quality. Makes expensive steak taste even better.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **ALCOHOL WILL FLAME**: When deglazing - pull pan from heat if concerned
- **WATCH REDUCTION**: Can go from perfect to burned in 30 seconds
- **USE REAL WINE**: Cooking wine tastes terrible - drink what you cook with
- **PAN MUST BE HOT**: Cold pan = boiled sauce, not reduced
- **BUTTER AT END ONLY**: Added early = separated, greasy sauce

## Equipment
- Skillet used for cooking steak (with fond/drippings)
- Whisk
- Measuring cup
- Timer

## Ingredients (makes ~1/2 cup, serves 2-3)
- Pan drippings from cooking steak
- 1 shallot, minced (or 2 garlic cloves)
- 1 cup dry red wine (Cab, Merlot, Pinot Noir)
- 1 cup beef stock (low-sodium)
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 tbsp cold butter, cubed
- Salt and pepper to taste
- Optional: 1 tsp Dijon mustard

## Method

### Step 1: Deglaze Pan (after cooking steak)
1. Remove steak from pan, let rest
2. Pour off all but 1 tbsp fat
3. Return pan to MEDIUM-HIGH heat
4. Add minced shallot
5. Cook 1 minute until softened
6. 🚨 **DEGLAZING**: Add wine, pull pan from heat briefly
7. **MAY FLAME** - this is normal, will burn off
8. Return to heat, scrape brown bits from pan with wooden spoon

### Step 2: First Reduction (5-7 minutes)
1. Add thyme sprigs
2. Let wine simmer/boil
3. **SET TIMER FOR 5 MINUTES**
4. Reduce until about 1/4 cup remains (syrupy)
5. 🚨 **WATCH CLOSELY** - should coat spoon
6. Smell should be wine-y but concentrated

### Step 3: Add Stock and Reduce (8-10 minutes)
1. Add beef stock
2. Increase heat to HIGH
3. **SET TIMER FOR 8 MINUTES**
4. Let boil, reduce by half
5. Should reduce to about 1/2 - 2/3 cup
6. 🚨 **TASTE**: Should be flavorful, slightly salty
7. Remove thyme sprigs

### Step 4: Mount with Butter (CRITICAL TECHNIQUE)
1. Remove pan from heat
2. Reduce to LOW heat
3. Add cold butter cubes one at a time
4. Whisk constantly as butter melts
5. 🚨 **WATCH TEMP**: Too hot = sauce breaks (greasy)
6. Should become glossy and slightly thickened
7. Add optional Dijon, whisk in

### Step 5: Season and Serve Immediately
1. Taste sauce
2. Add salt and pepper as needed
3. Should be savory, rich, slightly sweet
4. Serve immediately over steak
5. 🚨 **DON'T LET SIT**: Butter sauce will separate

## Perfect Sauce Indicators
- Glossy, not greasy
- Coats back of spoon
- Rich brown color
- Balanced wine/meat flavor
- Pourable but not watery

## Variations

### Peppercorn Cream Sauce
- After wine reduction, add 1/2 cup heavy cream
- Add 2 tbsp green peppercorns
- Skip butter mount

### Mushroom Red Wine
- Sauté 8 oz sliced mushrooms with shallots
- Proceed with recipe

### Port Wine Reduction
- Use port instead of red wine
- Slightly sweeter, great with beef

### Bordelaise (Classic French)
- Add bone marrow (scooped from marrow bones)
- Strain through fine sieve before mounting butter

## Troubleshooting
- **Sauce broke/greasy?** Butter added when too hot - whisk in ice cube to bring temp down
- **Too thin?** Didn't reduce enough - simmer longer
- **Too thick/syrupy?** Over-reduced - add splash of stock
- **Bitter?** Wine over-reduced or poor quality wine used
- **Burned bits?** Heat too high during reduction

## Pro Tips
- Make without steak pan: Use 2 tbsp butter instead of drippings to start
- Can use pan from any protein (chicken, pork)
- Freeze stock in ice cube trays for quick sauce making
- Better with homemade stock but store-bought works
- Red wine choice: Mid-range drinking wine, never "cooking wine"

## Make Ahead
- Can reduce wine and stock ahead
- Stop before butter mount
- Refrigerate up to 3 days
- Reheat gently, mount with butter when serving
