# Béarnaise Sauce

## Overview
French mother sauce - THE classic steak sauce. Rich, buttery, tarragon-forward. Tricky but worth mastering.

## 🚨 CRITICAL WARNINGS 🚨
- **TEMPERATURE CRITICAL**: Too hot = scrambled eggs, too cool = won't thicken
- **CONSTANT WHISKING**: Stop whisking = eggs cook unevenly and scramble
- **BUTTER TEMP**: Must be warm (not cold, not too hot)
- **MAKE LAST**: Serve immediately - doesn't hold well
- **PATIENCE REQUIRED**: Can't rush this - low and slow
- **HAVE RESCUE READY**: Ice cube or cold water to cool if overheating

## Equipment
- Small saucepan
- Heatproof bowl (for double boiler)
- Whisk (balloon whisk ideal)
- Measuring cups/spoons
- Thermometer helpful but not required
- Pot for double boiler

## Ingredients (makes ~1 cup, serves 4)

**For Reduction:**
- 1/4 cup white wine vinegar
- 1/4 cup dry white wine
- 2 tbsp minced shallots
- 1 tbsp fresh tarragon, chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
- 10 black peppercorns, crushed
- Pinch of salt

**For Sauce:**
- 3 egg yolks (room temperature)
- 3/4 cup (1.5 sticks) unsalted butter, melted and warm
- 1 tbsp fresh tarragon, minced
- 1 tbsp fresh chervil or parsley (optional)
- Salt to taste
- Pinch cayenne pepper
- 1-2 tsp lemon juice

## Method

### Step 1: Make Reduction (5 minutes)
1. Combine vinegar, wine, shallots, tarragon, peppercorns in small saucepan
2. Bring to simmer over medium heat
3. **SET TIMER FOR 4 MINUTES**
4. Reduce until about 2 tbsp liquid remains
5. Should be syrupy and aromatic
6. Strain through fine sieve
7. Let cool slightly (5 minutes)

### Step 2: Set Up Double Boiler
1. Fill pot with 2" water, bring to gentle simmer
2. Place heatproof bowl over pot (shouldn't touch water)
3. Reduce heat to LOW - water should barely simmer
4. 🚨 **NOT BOILING** - steam only
5. Have ice cube ready in case sauce overheats

### Step 3: Start Emulsion (5-7 minutes)
1. Add egg yolks and reduction to bowl
2. Whisk constantly (don't stop!)
3. 🚨 **WATCH BOTTOM OF BOWL** - if you see any specks forming (scrambling), immediately:
   - Pull bowl off heat
   - Drop in ice cube OR add 1 tbsp cold water
   - Whisk vigorously
4. Whisk until mixture thickens and becomes pale yellow
5. Should be able to see whisk trails
6. Should coat back of spoon
7. **THIS TAKES 5-7 MINUTES** - be patient

### Step 4: Add Butter (Slowly!)
1. Remove bowl from heat
2. Add warm melted butter VERY SLOWLY
3. Start with drops, whisking constantly
4. As emulsion forms, increase to thin stream
5. 🚨 **TOO FAST = BROKEN SAUCE**
6. Whisk constantly - should thicken and become smooth
7. Should look like thick, glossy mayonnaise

### Step 5: Finish and Season
1. Whisk in fresh tarragon and herbs
2. Add salt to taste
3. Add pinch of cayenne
4. Add lemon juice (brightens flavor)
5. 🚨 **TASTE**: Should be rich, buttery, tangy, with tarragon flavor
6. Adjust seasoning as needed

### Step 6: Hold and Serve (30 min max)
**To Hold:**
- Keep in bowl over warm (not hot) water
- Whisk occasionally
- 🚨 **DON'T OVERHEAT** - will break

**To Serve:**
- Spoon over hot steak immediately
- Serve extra in sauceboat

## Perfect Béarnaise Indicators
- Smooth, no lumps or specks
- Thick enough to coat spoon
- Glossy, pale yellow
- Strong tarragon flavor
- Rich and buttery
- NOT: Broken, curdled, or runny

## Rescuing Broken Béarnaise

**If Sauce Breaks (looks curdled/separated):**

1. **Method 1 - New Yolk:**
   - In clean bowl, whisk 1 egg yolk with 1 tsp water
   - Slowly whisk broken sauce into new yolk
   - Should come together

2. **Method 2 - Ice Water:**
   - Add 1 tsp ice water to broken sauce
   - Whisk vigorously off heat
   - May need 2-3 tsp total

3. **Method 3 - Blender:**
   - Pour into blender
   - Blend on low, slowly add 1 tbsp warm water
   - Should emulsify

## Troubleshooting
- **Scrambled eggs?** Heat too high - start over (can't fix)
- **Too thick?** Whisk in warm water, 1 tsp at a time
- **Too thin?** Didn't cook yolks enough or added butter too fast
- **Broken/separated?** See rescue methods above
- **No tarragon flavor?** Used dried instead of fresh - add more fresh

## Variations

### Hollandaise (Parent Sauce)
- Omit tarragon, shallots, peppercorns
- Use just lemon juice instead of reduction
- Perfect for eggs Benedict

### Choron Sauce
- Add 2 tbsp tomato paste at end
- Great with beef or fish

### Paloise Sauce
- Replace tarragon with mint
- Perfect for lamb

## Pro Tips
- Room temp eggs emulsify better (set out 30 min ahead)
- Can use clarified butter (more stable, less likely to break)
- Practice makes perfect - this is an advanced sauce
- Make Hollandaise first (easier) to learn technique
- Fresh tarragon is NON-NEGOTIABLE - dried doesn't work well
- Can make reduction day ahead, refrigerate

## Timing for Dinner
- Make reduction ahead
- Make sauce just before serving
- Can hold 20-30 min max over warm water
- If longer, plan to remake (takes only 10 min once you're practiced)
